[✨🎬] Play hosted workshop tracks with one clock, automatic speed and synchronized Git context

- Build the participant player on [the published hosted recording](2026-09-0890-workshop-hosted-multitrack-recordings.md) and [its timeline metadata](2026-09-0880-studio-workshop-timeline-metadata.md). Preserve the existing YouTube player for YouTube-only workshops. Integrate with `WorkshopStage.tsx`, the existing wrap-up/replay flow, supplementary video materials and `WorkshopRepositoryPanel.tsx`; no separate workshop lifecycle or membership policy.
- Show four tabs: `Auto`, `Editor`, `Aplikace`, `Kamera`. Editor, application and camera show their corresponding full-frame source. `Auto` chooses the relevant screen source from time-stamped scene choices (or an explicitly configured default) and overlays the camera in the bottom-right picture-in-picture. If a source is absent/gapped, show its state and use a documented available fallback in Auto, without displaying a stale frame. Changing tabs never resets playback position, speed or playing state. At most one audio feed is audible, selected consistently; no echo from simultaneous camera and screen audio.
- Use one authoritative session playhead for all available tracks, including scene switches and the camera overlay. Coordinate asynchronous seeks/buffering and drift correction; do not rely on independent native video controls. Provide play/pause, seekable timeline, current/duration time, mute/volume, fullscreen and speed choices including `Auto`, with keyboard, touch and mobile support. No visible captions/subtitles over the video.
- Render event markers on the timeline with an accessible label/detail. Distinguish `active` from `automatic-coding` intervals visually. In `Auto` speed mode play active/unclassified time at 1× and automatic-coding ranges at 10×, changing precisely at interval boundaries in either direction after seek; manual speed stays fixed. If the platform cannot sustain 10× cleanly, implement a predictable accelerated navigation strategy that preserves the common playhead and document its behavior. Do not change the `Auto` view tab when the `Auto` speed mode changes.
- Highlight the commit associated with the current playhead in the existing workshop repository panel/graph and offer a link to that SHA. The mapping uses the recording's starting commit and anchored timeline, not a guess based on video duration or the current live repository HEAD. A missing/deleted commit or absent repository yields a clear unavailable state without breaking playback; seeking and tab changes update the selection together. Keep manual graph exploration possible without causing the video to seek unexpectedly.
- During the workshop, free viewers join at the server-authoritative live position determined by workshop start, recording offset and actual available media. They may switch tabs and playback controls permitted for live viewing, but cannot seek, pause into a replay, switch speed to get ahead/behind, or request earlier/later media ranges through the API; reconnect jumps to the current allowed position. A paid member may seek anywhere available. After the workshop, preserve the existing paid replay gate and identical wrap-up screen for paying and non-paying users. Authorize time-window requests server-side, including byte/rendition requests, rather than only disabling the slider. Define buffering and not-yet-uploaded live segments honestly; do not imply a completed upload is a live stream without a delivery path.
- Acceptance criteria:
    - Switching among all four tabs and seeking keeps screen, camera, event markers and selected commit at the same session timestamp. Test delayed buffers and gaps; measure alignment against the studio's documented tolerance.
    - Auto speed changes 1× → 10× → 1× at annotated boundaries, including after seeks and across appended takes; manual speed does not vary.
    - A free live viewer can switch tracks but cannot replay or fetch past/future protected segments; a paying member can seek. Both see the existing wrap-up and the proper replay/teaser after the event.
    - YouTube-only events and events whose primary stage is presentation or repository retain their current behavior and access rules.
- Keep in mind the DRY _(don't repeat yourself)_ principle. Share clock, timing and authorization with the upload and metadata PRDs.
- Do an analysis of the current functionality before you start implementing.
- Add the changes into the [changelog](../changelog/_current-preversion.md).
